City overview

Hartford's south side and how we cover it

Hartford is a large city, and we focus on the south side, the part nearest Wethersfield. Neighborhoods there include South End, South Green, Southwest and Barry Square, according to the neighborhood names used in the Connecticut Data Haven's Hartford profile. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock for these streets.

Hartford is also an MDC member, so water and sewer mains are MDC's. That keeps one rule simple for you. A problem in the street is MDC's to fix, and a problem inside your property is generally the owner's, with MDC able to confirm the boundary.

Older buildings, older pipes

A 2017 report drawing on Governing magazine data found that 66 percent of Hartford's housing was built before 1960 and 34 percent before 1940. A house or building of that age can have galvanized or cast iron pipe, clay sewer laterals and layered repairs from many decades.

We do not assume what a building has. We look at the supply, the drain and the water heater, we tell you what we find, and we separate what is urgent from what can wait. Our sewer camera inspection is often the first step when a south Hartford drain keeps backing up.

Multi-family buildings

Two and three family houses share stacks and basements, so one leak can reach several units. Tell us which unit and floor you are in.

Basement drains

A floor drain that gurgles in a heavy rain often points to the main, not the fixture.

Aging water heaters

Tanks in older buildings are often past their prime, and a leaking tank floods a shared basement.

Old supply lines

Pinholes in old pipe show up as stains on a ceiling below.

Burst pipe repair

Winter adds its own pattern. A burst pipe in an unheated hall or a frozen line along an outside wall can soak the apartments below, and a leak in a shared basement can reach several tenants. Call as soon as you see water, close the shutoff if you can reach it, and tell us the floor and the unit so the plumber arrives knowing where to start.
