Heat Pump Water Heater Problems: Wethersfield, CT Guide

Heat pump water heaters fail in ways a standard tank does not. Here are the common faults and the checks that are safe to do.

Wethersfield guideLocal facts

Written by the Wethersfield 24/7 Plumbing team. Published and updated November 14, 2026.

A heat pump water heater moves heat from the surrounding air into the water, like a refrigerator in reverse. It uses much less electricity than a standard electric tank. It also has a fan, a condensate drain and a sealed refrigerant system, so its problems look different from a tank that simply stopped heating.

Before you call, a few checks take minutes and fix a surprising share of calls. This guide lists them, explains what the usual faults mean and tells you where a plumber or a refrigeration specialist is needed.

How the unit works

Most heat pump water heaters are hybrids. They have a heat pump on top and a regular electric element as a backup. The unit chooses the mode based on demand and settings. It also cools and dehumidifies the room it sits in, which is why it needs space and air flow.

Checks you can do safely

1

Check the mode

Make sure the unit is not set to vacation or off, and that it is in hybrid or heat pump mode.

2

Clean the air filter

A clogged filter chokes air flow and makes the unit work harder or shut down.

3

Check the breaker

A tripped breaker is a common reason for no hot water.

4

Inspect the condensate drain

A blocked condensate line can trip a safety switch. Look for water around the base.

Common faults

Error codes on the display

The manual lists what each code means. Many are airflow, temperature or sensor faults.

Loud fan or compressor noise

A dirty filter or a loose panel can cause it. A grinding compressor needs a specialist.

Water around the base

A clogged condensate drain, a loose fitting or a leaking tank are the usual causes.

Warm but not hot water

The unit may be in a mode that does not use the element, or the upper element has failed.

Check the space, not just the unit

A heat pump heater pulls heat from the room. A very small closet, a room that is always cold or poor air flow cuts its output. The fix may be to open a door or to add a vent, not to repair the unit.

What makes heat pump units different to service

A standard electric tank has two elements, two thermostats and very little else. A heat pump unit adds a compressor, an evaporator coil, a fan, temperature sensors and a control board. Each can fail on its own, and the control board often hides the cause behind a short error code. That is why the first step is always to read the code and the manual.

The unit also moves a lot of air, and it chills the room as it works. A basement that was warm becomes cool, and a damp one can build up condensation. The condensate drain handles the water that forms on the coil. If the drain line is kinked, clogged with algae or routed uphill with no pump, water backs up and can trip a float switch that shuts the unit down.

Keeping the unit healthy

  • Clean or replace the air filter every few months, or as the manual says.
  • Keep at least the clearance the manufacturer lists around the unit.
  • Flush the tank once a year to remove sediment.
  • Check the anode rod on the schedule in the manual.
  • Listen for changes in the fan or compressor sound.

These checks take minutes and catch most problems early. Because the unit uses its backup element when the heat pump cannot keep up, a rise in your electric bill with no change in hot water use can be the first sign that the heat pump side is not running.

Keep the manual and the warranty papers where you can find them. Many heat pump units carry a longer warranty on the sealed system than on the tank or the elements, and the terms often require service records. A dated note of each filter cleaning and each repair makes any later claim simpler.

When you call, have the model number, the error code and a note of what you already checked. A technician who knows those three things can bring the right parts and often fixes the problem in a single visit.

Code and permit note

Parts and filter care need no permit. Replacing the unit does touch the plumbing code, which in Connecticut is the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted by the 2022 State Building Code, and electrical changes have their own rules.
