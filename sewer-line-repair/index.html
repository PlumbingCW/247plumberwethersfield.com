A sewer line that has failed can send wastewater into a basement, so the line is answered 24/7. While you wait, stop using every drain, do not flush, and keep people and pets away from any standing water. Do not run a washer or a dishwasher.

A backup that keeps coming back after a cleaning usually means the pipe itself is damaged. Repair is the work that fixes the pipe, not just the clog.

Sewer backup: what to do first

Safe steps while you wait

Sewage can carry bacteria and should be treated with care.

  • Stop all water use, including toilets, sinks, dishwashers and washers.
  • Avoid contact with the water. Wear waterproof boots and gloves if you must enter the area.
  • Keep electrical equipment away from standing water. Do not use a wet vacuum on sewage.
  • Call us. If the backup is in the street main, we will tell you and point you to MDC.

Signs your sewer line is failing

Backups in the lowest drains

A basement floor drain or a shower that bubbles or fills is the first place a blocked line shows.

Several fixtures slow at once

A clog in one fixture is local. Slow drains across the house point to the main line.

Gurgling and odors

Air pushed through a partially blocked line comes out through traps and vents as noise and smell.

Wet or sunken ground

A soggy patch or a dip in the lawn above the line can mean the pipe has broken and is leaking into the soil.

How we find the problem

A camera on a flexible cable goes down the line and shows what is happening, from roots to a crushed section to a pipe that has shifted. We note the distance from the cleanout so the repair is accurate, and we can locate the spot at the surface. You see the video before you approve anything.

The camera also tells us whether a cleaning will be enough. A line with a little root growth may need only a jetting. A line with a break or a belly where water pools needs a repair. See our sewer inspection page for what a camera run involves.

Wethersfield sewers and your lateral

MDC owns and operates the sewer mains in Wethersfield. Its Clean Water Project says it has lined more than 20 miles of sewer pipe in town and rehabilitated or replaced about 190 sanitary manholes. MDC also says most of the 98 miles of sewer mains are more than 50 years old, with some from 1914.

That work is on the mains in the street. The pipe from your house to the main is often the owner's to maintain, so confirm the boundary with MDC for your property. If the camera shows the problem is in the main, MDC handles it. If it is on your side, we repair it. The camera tells you which.

Roots, age and older homes

About 27 percent of Wethersfield's housing was built before 1950. Homes of that era can have clay, cast iron or other older pipe in the lateral, and joints on those materials let roots in. We do not assume the pipe type. The camera shows it, and the repair follows from what we see.

Code and permit note

Sewer work is among the most regulated plumbing there is. Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which sets rules for building sewers, cleanouts and slope. Permit rules vary with the work, and the Building Department can tell you what applies to your address.
