When a tank splits open, the first minutes decide how much water ends up on the floor, so the line is answered 24/7. While you wait, close the cold water valve above the tank, switch off the breaker or turn the gas control to off, and open a hot tap upstairs to ease the pressure.

Replacement is the right call when the tank shell leaks, when rust has eaten through, or when the repair would cost more than it buys. We help you choose a replacement that fits your house and tell you what the job involves before any work starts.

Act first

The tank failed tonight: your first moves

Before the plumber arrives

A failed tank can hold forty gallons or more, so contain it early.

  • Close the cold supply valve on the pipe above the heater. If it will not turn, shut the main valve.
  • Cut power at the breaker for an electric unit. For gas, set the control to off.
  • Move stored items away from the base and use towels or a wet vacuum on shallow water. Avoid the vacuum if the floor is soaked near outlets.
  • If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 before you call us.

Signs a tank should be replaced, not repaired

A leak from the tank body

Water seeping from the shell, not a fitting, means the steel liner has failed. No repair fixes that.

Rust-colored hot water

Brown water from hot taps only, with clear cold water, points to corrosion inside the tank.

A unit that is well past its years

Many tanks last about ten to fifteen years. A tank past that range can fail without warning.

Repeated repairs

A second or third repair in a short span usually costs more than the unit is worth.

Choosing the replacement

Size comes first. A tank that is too small runs out of hot water, and one that is too large wastes energy keeping water hot. We ask how many people live in the house, how many baths and whether a soaking tub or a second shower runs often. Then we match the capacity to the answer.

Next comes fuel and venting. A gas tank needs a proper flue and combustion air. An electric tank needs the right circuit. We check both before we order. If you want to consider a tankless or heat pump model, we explain what each needs in your house. See our tankless water heater page for the on-demand option.

Replacing a heater in a Wethersfield house

Wethersfield homes are mostly single-family with basements, and the water heater usually sits near the main shutoff. That makes the swap straightforward, but a basement with a finished ceiling or a narrow stair affects how the old tank comes out and the new one goes in. We ask about access when you call so the plumber arrives with the right tools.

MDC says its water has a hardness of about 1 grain per gallon, which is soft. In a soft-water house, a replacement tank is less likely to be undermined by scale and more likely to be limited by its own anode and tank life. Ask us to check the anode on any new tank at an annual visit, since that rod is what protects the steel.

Code and permit note

A new water heater is a code matter, not just a purchase. Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which covers relief valves, discharge piping and the drain pan where one is required. Ask the Wethersfield Building Department whether a replacement needs a permit.
