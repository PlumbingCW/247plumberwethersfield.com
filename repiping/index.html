Repiping replaces the water supply pipes in a house, either the whole system or a major section. It is a larger job than a repair, and it is worth doing when pipes have reached the end of their life and spot repairs keep returning.

This is planned work. If a pipe is bursting now, see burst pipe repair and call our 24/7 line first.

Signs your pipes are wearing out

Pinhole leaks

A pinhole that appears in one pipe is often the first of several. Corrosion thins the wall from the inside.

Rusty or brown water

Water that runs brown from every tap, especially after sitting, can come from corroding iron or steel pipe.

Low pressure throughout the house

Mineral and rust buildup narrows old pipes until flow drops at every fixture.

Repeated repairs

Three repairs in a few years is a pattern, and it usually costs more than a planned replacement.

What your pipes are made of

Older houses can have galvanized steel supply pipe, which rusts from the inside. Copper has served well for decades but can develop pinholes. Newer systems use PEX, which is flexible and resists corrosion and freezing better than rigid pipe. We identify what you have before we recommend anything.

A pipe material is not good or bad by itself. What matters is its condition and how it was installed. We show you the pipe where we can, and we explain what we see.

How a repipe goes

Most repipes run new lines through basements, crawl spaces and, where needed, through walls and ceilings. We plan routes to limit the amount of wall that has to be opened, and we explain what patching is part of the job and what is not. You can usually keep the house in use, though water is off during parts of each day.

In a house with a basement, much of the work happens below the main floor, which keeps the disruption down. Upper floors can often be reached through closets and utility chases.

Repiping an older Wethersfield house

About 27 percent of Wethersfield's housing was built before 1950, and the 1950s added roughly a house a day. Homes of either era can have original pipes that are now seventy years old or more. A house that has never had its supply lines replaced is a candidate for a survey, even if nothing is wrong yet.

MDC states that its water has a hardness of about 1 grain per gallon and an average pH of 7.41, according to its 2025 water quality report. Soft, near-neutral water is generally kinder to pipes than water that is hard or acidic, but it does not stop old pipe from aging. We look at the pipe, not the assumption.

Partial or whole house

A section repipe, such as a bathroom group or a run to the kitchen, can be the right call when the rest of the system is sound. A full repipe makes sense when the whole system is the same age and condition. We tell you which approach fits, and we give you a price for each so you can compare. If a leak is your trigger, our leak detection service can find it first.

Code and permit note

A repipe replaces most of the supply system, which makes it a permitted project in most cases. The state's 2022 building code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), and permit rules vary with the work. Call the Wethersfield Building Department at 860-721-2840 before the walls are opened.
