Where Griswoldville is

The town's 2013 plan places Griswoldville along Two Stone Brook and Wolcott Hill Road, where a trolley line once ran to Hartford. The plan also places a grist mill on Two Stone Brook in the period from 1650 to 1700. A local history page notes that the Griswoldville Chapel was built in 1872 as a chapel and Sunday school, because the trip to First Church was hard in bad weather.

We have not found an official boundary for Griswoldville, so we describe it by those two anchor features. Real estate sites also use Griswoldville and Wolcott Hill as neighborhood labels, and the two overlap. That is why this one page covers both and why we describe a job by its street address.

Put plainly, the sources tell us this is a long-settled part of town with homes from several decades. They do not tell us what is inside the walls of your house. We would rather say that than guess.

Plumbing work in Connecticut follows the State Building Code, which adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), so a replacement is judged against a published standard and not against what the old pipe happened to be.

Planning by the question and not by the era

Water heater age

The data plate shows the manufacture date. An older tank is worth planning to replace before it fails and floods a basement. See water heater repair.

Basement moisture

Damp walls after a storm, a musty smell or a sump pump that runs constantly are worth a visit. See sump pump repair for what a check covers.

Slow drains in more than one fixture

When a sink, tub and floor drain all slow down together, the shared line is the likely place to look. See sewer line cleaning.

Pressure that changed

Low pressure at one faucet points to that fixture. Low pressure everywhere points to the main valve, the regulator or the service line.

The brook, basements and the sewer

MDC's Clean Water Project page for Wethersfield says heavy rain raises the flow in the sewers from about 1.7 million gallons a day in dry weather to about 9.1 million. It says that surge can back sewage into basements, and it names homeowner connections such as downspouts, sump pumps and yard drains as one source of the extra water.

If you live near the brook, check where your sump pump and downspouts discharge, and ask MDC what is allowed on your property before changing anything. For the flood zone status of a specific address, FEMA's Flood Map Service Center lets you look it up. We have not assessed flood risk for any Griswoldville street.

If water shows up in the basement

1

Stay out of standing water

Water near outlets or the panel is a shock risk. Keep children and pets away.

2

Look for the source

Clear water from a pipe or tank is a plumbing problem. Gray or smelly water from a floor drain may be a sewer problem.

3

Stop what you can

Close the main shutoff if a pipe is leaking. If sewage is backing up, stop using drains and report it to MDC.

Questions about plumbing in Griswoldville

They overlap. The town plan places Griswoldville along Two Stone Brook and Wolcott Hill Road, and real estate sites use both names. This page covers both, and we go by your street address.

We have no evidence about specific streets and will not guess. If your basement takes on water after rain, call and we will look at the cause, whether it is groundwater, a failed sump pump or a drainage problem.

MDC maintains the sewer main in the street. The pipe from your house to the main is generally the owner's. Report the backup to MDC, and a camera inspection shows which side the blockage is on.

Ask MDC before changing any discharge. Its Clean Water Project page names homeowner connections such as downspouts and sump pumps as a source of the extra water that stresses the sewers.

Check the data plate for the manufacture date and look for rust, moisture at the base or inconsistent hot water. Call and we will explain whether to repair or replace.
