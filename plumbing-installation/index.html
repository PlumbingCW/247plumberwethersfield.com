Plumbing installation covers the work that adds or changes what is in your house. That can be a toilet, a sink, a tub, a washing machine hookup or a new line to a laundry room. Planned work gives us the time to do it properly and to match your plans.

If something is leaking right now, see faucet and sink repair or burst pipe repair and call our 24/7 line.

What we install

Fixtures

Toilets, sinks, faucets, tubs and showers, set level and tested for leaks.

Appliance hookups

Washers, dishwashers, ice makers and utility sinks connected with the right valves and drains.

New lines and moves

Supply and drain lines for a new bath, a basement bath or a relocated laundry.

Outdoor connections

Frost-proof hose bibbs and lines for outdoor use, with the shutoff where you can reach it.

Planning a remodel with a plumber

The earlier a plumber sees a remodel, the cheaper it is. Moving a toilet a few feet can mean cutting a slab or a floor framing. Moving it back by the old stack usually costs far less. We look at the plans, the stacks and the vents, and we tell you where the cost is before you commit.

We also work with other trades. A plumber's rough-in has to be done before walls close and tile goes down, and we coordinate the schedule so your project does not stall.

Basement baths and drains below the sewer line

A basement bath may sit below the level of the sewer line, which means waste has to be lifted by a pump. That adds equipment and maintenance, and it should be planned from the start. We survey the line depth before we quote a basement bathroom.

Water softeners and filters

People often ask about softeners and whole-house filters. MDC states that its water has a hardness of about 1 grain per gallon, which is soft, so a softener to remove hardness is rarely needed in Wethersfield. A filter can still suit a household that wants to change taste or reduce a specific substance, and we can plumb one in. We recommend you test your tap before buying, since water quality varies at the tap.

MDC reports that its water has a pH averaging 7.41 and is treated with chlorine and fluoride. If you have a question about a specific substance, MDC's Water Quality Laboratory can be reached at 860-278-7850, extension 3904.

Permits and inspections

Permit rules vary by the work. The Wethersfield Building Department, at 505 Silas Deane Highway, accepts permit applications through its online portal and inspects plumbing work. Its phone number is 860-721-2840. We tell you what the work involves so that your question to the department is specific.

Older houses and new fixtures

About 27 percent of Wethersfield homes were built before 1950, so a new fixture often connects to older pipe. We check the shutoff valves, the supply lines and the drain at each connection, and we replace the small parts that age first. If the old supply is in poor condition, we tell you and may suggest repiping as a longer-term plan.

Code and permit note

New fixtures and new lines are the core of what the plumbing code regulates. Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which covers fixture clearances, pipe sizing, venting and shutoffs. Ask the Building Department what your project needs.
