The water service line is the pipe that brings water from the street main to your house. When it leaks or breaks, the signs are a soggy yard, low pressure or a meter that spins with every tap closed. Our line is answered 24/7. While you wait, close the main valve at the house and stay away from any pooled water near electric equipment.

Who repairs the line depends on where the break is. We help you work out which side of the meter it is on, because that decides whether MDC or you are responsible.

A service line leak: what to do first

Steps while you wait

A broken line under pressure can wash out soil and create sinkholes.

  • Close the main valve in the basement, usually near the meter.
  • Note the meter reading and take a photo of any wet ground.
  • If water is pooling at the street or the curb, it may be on MDC's side. MDC lists its Command Center at (860) 278-7850, press 1, to report a main break or service leak.
  • Call us. We will help you work out where the break is.

Signs a service line is failing

A wet or sunken spot in the yard

Water that stays at the surface in dry weather often comes from a buried pipe.

Low pressure at every fixture

If pressure drops in the whole house, the problem is often at the line or the main valve.

A meter that moves with everything off

A closed house with a moving meter means water is leaving somewhere. The line is one of the places to check.

Discolored water

Brown or cloudy water can mean the line is disturbed or corroded. If it persists, report it to MDC.

How we deal with a service line problem

We begin by identifying where the line enters the house, what it is made of and where the meter and the shutoff sit. We then test the line with pressure and listening equipment to find the leak. If the break is on the street side of the meter, we tell you and you report it to MDC. If it is on your side, we repair or replace it.

A short repair works when the pipe is otherwise sound. A line that is old, corroded or repeatedly leaking is often better replaced. We explain the choices and the costs, and you choose.

MDC's work on Silas Deane Highway and what it means for your line

MDC began replacing water mains, services and meter pits along Silas Deane Highway in April 2025, with completion projected for August 2026. The work covers about 1.3 miles from Parsonage Street in Rocky Hill to Mill Street in Wethersfield, plus part of Town Line Road. Check themdc.org for the current status of the project.

If you live along the corridor, a new connection on MDC's side can change the pressure or stir up sediment on yours. A pressure check and a look at the first few feet of pipe inside the house is a reasonable precaution after nearby work.

Lead and materials

MDC's 2025 water quality report says the district uses no lead pipes in its distribution system and that it completed its service line inventory in 2024. The pipe on your property can be a different matter in an older house. We identify the material when we work on the line, and we tell you what we see so you can follow up with MDC if you have questions.

Code and permit note

The service line is the longest pipe on the property, and work on it is regulated. Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which sets material and depth expectations, and MDC controls the connection at the main. Confirm both before anyone digs.
