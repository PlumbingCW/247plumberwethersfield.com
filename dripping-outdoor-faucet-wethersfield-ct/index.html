Dripping Outdoor Faucet? Fixes for Wethersfield, CT Homes

A dripping hose bibb wastes water and can freeze a pipe inside the wall. Most drips are a simple fix.

Wethersfield guideLocal facts
Talk to a plumber
(860) 397-8103Call now

Written by the Wethersfield 24/7 Plumbing team. Published and updated November 14, 2026.

A dripping outdoor faucet is usually a worn washer or a loose packing nut. Both are cheap and quick to fix. The bigger risk comes in winter, when a hose left attached can freeze water inside the faucet and split the pipe behind the wall, so the fix is often as much about winter prep as the drip.

This guide explains why outdoor faucets drip, how to repair a basic one, how to shut one down for winter and when a faucet should be replaced.

Why outdoor faucets drip

Drip from the spout

A worn washer or a damaged valve seat lets water slip past when the faucet is closed.

Leak around the handle

A loose or worn packing nut lets water seep up the stem when the faucet is on.

Drip inside the house after a freeze

A frozen faucet may have cracked the pipe inside the wall, and the drip shows up in the basement.

Low pressure at the hose

Mineral buildup or a partly closed shutoff can cut the flow.

Fix a leak at the handle

If water seeps around the stem when the faucet is on, tighten the packing nut with a wrench, a quarter turn at a time. If it still leaks, remove the handle and nut, wrap a few turns of packing string or Teflon tape around the stem and reassemble. This repair takes minutes.

Fix a drip from the spout

1

Shut off the water

Close the interior shutoff for the line if there is one, then open the faucet to drain it.

2

Take it apart

Remove the handle, the packing nut and the stem.

3

Replace the washer

Swap the rubber washer at the end of the stem and check the seat for pitting.

4

Reassemble

Put it back together, open the shutoff and test.

Winter shutdown that prevents a burst

1

Remove the hose

Disconnect hoses and splitters from every outdoor faucet before the first freeze.

2

Close the shutoff

Find the interior shutoff for the line, usually in the basement near the wall the faucet is on, and close it.

3

Drain the line

Open the outdoor faucet so the water drains out of the pipe.

4

Cover it

A foam cover helps on very cold nights but does not replace draining.

Frost-proof faucets still need the hose off

A frost-proof faucet has a long stem so the valve sits inside the warm house. It only works if water can drain out of the stem, and a hose left attached traps water and defeats it.

Finding the shutoff in a Wethersfield house

MDC's page on reporting a main break or service leak notes that the shutoff for an outdoor faucet is usually within about 3 feet of the interior basement wall, near where the faucet is mounted outside.

Look along the basement wall at the spot that matches the faucet's outside position. You are looking for a small valve with a handle or a screw cap on a copper line. Closing it in the fall and draining the faucet is one of the simplest ways to protect the pipe.

Source type: MDC, report a main break or service leak page.

Frost proof versus standard faucets

A standard hose bibb has its valve right at the outside wall, so the water sitting behind the valve is exposed to the cold. A frost proof faucet has a long stem, often six to twelve inches or more, so the valve seat sits inside the warm part of the house. Water drains out of the stem when you shut it off, which keeps the pipe from freezing.

Frost proof faucets still fail when the hose is left on, because water cannot drain and freezes in the stem. They also need to slope slightly downward so the water runs out. A faucet that was installed level or tilted up can hold water even without a hose. If you find a split in a frost proof stem in spring, the install angle is worth checking before you replace it.

What to do if a pipe has frozen

1

Close the interior shutoff

If water is leaking inside, close the shutoff or the main valve right away.

2

Open the faucet

Leave it open so the thawing water has somewhere to go.

3

Thaw gently

Use a hair dryer on low, working from the faucet toward the pipe. Never use a torch or an open flame.

4

Look for damage

Check the pipe and the wall around it for splits, wet insulation and drips once the water flows.

5

Call for help if it leaks

A split pipe needs a repair before the water goes back on.

Code and permit note

The 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), adopted by Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code, asks for backflow protection on hose connections. A vacuum breaker on an outdoor spigot is a small part that keeps hose water from flowing back into your supply.
