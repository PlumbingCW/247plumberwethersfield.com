Storm and a dead pump: what to do first

Steps while the water rises

Water in a basement can reach outlets and appliances quickly.

  • Do not step into standing water if cords, outlets or the panel are near it. Cut power at the panel only if it is dry and you can reach it safely.
  • Check that the pump is plugged in and the GFCI outlet has not tripped.
  • Lift the float by hand. If the pump starts, the float or the switch is the problem.
  • Call us. If the pit is overflowing, a bucket brigade or a wet vacuum can buy time.

Why sump pumps fail

A stuck or failed float

The float tells the pump when to start. If it jams against the pit wall or fails, the pump will not run.

A dead motor

Pumps that run often wear out. An old pump may fail exactly when the storm hits.

A clogged inlet or a failed check valve

Debris blocks the intake, and a failed check valve lets water run back into the pit.

Power loss

Storms cause outages. Without a battery backup, a pump will not run when the power is out.

What we do

We test the pump, the float, the check valve and the discharge line. A stuck float, a clogged inlet or a loose connection is a short repair. A dead motor means a new pump, and we match the size to your pit and your water table. We also check that the discharge line sends water away from the house.

For many houses, a battery backup or a water-powered backup is a sensible addition. We explain what each offers and what it costs, and you decide.

Why Wethersfield basements take water

MDC's Clean Water Project says that in heavy rain, sewer flow in Wethersfield rises from about 1.7 million gallons a day to about 9.1 million, and that overflows can reach basements and Wethersfield Cove. A sump pump deals with groundwater, which is a different problem from a sewer backup, but both show up in wet basements, and we help you tell them apart.

The same program describes removing homeowner drainage connections, such as downspouts, sump pumps and yard drains, from the sanitary sewer. If your pump discharges into a drain that goes to the sewer, ask MDC or the Wethersfield Building Department what is allowed before you change anything. A pump that discharges outside, away from the foundation, is the usual approach. If you live along Two Stone Brook or Wolcott Hill Road, see our Griswoldville plumbing guide.

Where the water goes

A discharge line that ends too close to the house simply sends water back to the foundation. We look at the exit point, the slope and the winter risk of ice in the line. The best pump in the world cannot help if its discharge freezes shut.

Code and permit note

A sump pump swap is usually maintenance, while a new pit or a changed discharge is plumbing work. Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), and MDC separately asks homeowners to keep sump discharge out of the sanitary sewer.
