After-hours emergency: first steps for a business

Protect staff, stock and the building

A commercial leak can reach a neighbor's unit or the floor below.

  • Close the nearest shutoff valve, or the main if you cannot find it. Know where yours is before you need it.
  • Keep staff and customers away from wet floors and any electrical equipment in the area.
  • Contact your landlord or property manager if you share the building.
  • Take photos of the damage and the source for your insurer.

How a commercial call is different

A business call usually has more people affected and less time to spare. We ask about the building, who owns what, and whether the closing time or a tenant's schedule is a factor. We also ask about access, such as a key box or a locked back door.

We keep the price clear. If the repair affects other tenants or requires coordination with a landlord, we say so. We write what we did so you have a record for your landlord or insurer.

Kitchens, grease and the drains below

Restaurants and cafes put heavy loads of grease and food solids into their drains. A hydro jetting visit cleans the wall of the pipe, and a regular schedule can cut the number of backups. We can talk through grease interceptor and backflow questions on the phone, and we tell you if a specialist is needed.

Wethersfield's commercial streets

The town's 2013 plan describes Silas Deane Highway and the Berlin Turnpike as its commercial corridors, and our base on Silas Deane Highway means those two are close to dispatch. MDC is also replacing water mains, services and meter pits along Silas Deane Highway, with completion projected for August 2026. A business on that stretch should watch for pressure changes and check themdc.org for status.

The Wethersfield Building Department, at 505 Silas Deane Highway, issues permits and inspects plumbing work, and its number is 860-721-2840. Commercial jobs often involve permit questions, so we explain the work in plain terms so you can ask the department directly.

Leaks you cannot see

A water bill that climbs in a shop or office points to a hidden leak, and commercial leaks can run for weeks in a closed building. Our leak detection service traces them without opening more of the building than needed.

Code and permit note

Commercial work carries more rules than residential work. Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which sets fixture counts, backflow protection and grease handling. A business owner should expect permits and inspections on most changes.
