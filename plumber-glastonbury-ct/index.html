Local plumbing

Glastonbury's water comes from more than one place

Glastonbury's town website says its drinking water is sourced from the Metropolitan District Commission, the Town of Manchester, community wells and private wells. Which one serves you depends on your street, and that decides who to call when something goes wrong.

If your home is on a public system, a main problem belongs to that supplier. If you have a private well, the house plumbing is ours to fix and the water quality is a job for a state-certified lab and the town's Health Department. The health department suggests testing a private well every five years, or sooner if you notice a change in water quality or quantity.

Sewer and septic in Glastonbury

The town owns and operates its public sanitary sewer system under its Water Pollution Control Authority, and the town says sewer service reaches only certain areas. That means a house may be on public sewer or may be on a septic system, and the right plumbing advice differs. Extensions, new connections and changes to existing connections need approval from the town's authority.

When a drain backs up in Glastonbury, we start by asking which kind of system you have. A backup on a public sewer connection points to the lateral or the main. A backup on septic can point to the tank or the field. We tell you what is ours to fix and what needs a septic specialist.
