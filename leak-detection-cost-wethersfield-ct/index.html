Leak Detection Cost in Wethersfield, CT: What Affects Price

Leak detection pricing depends on how hard the leak is to reach and which tools it takes. Here is what moves the number.

Written by the Wethersfield 24/7 Plumbing team. Published and updated November 14, 2026.

There is no flat price for finding a leak, because leaks differ so much. A visible drip under a sink is a quick job. A slow leak under a slab or in a wall is not. We do not publish a fixed figure because it would mislead you, and this guide explains what changes the cost so you can judge a quote.

The good news is that finding a leak is usually cheaper than guessing. Opening walls at random costs far more than a few hours with the right equipment.

What drives the price of leak detection

Where the leak is

A leak in an exposed basement line is simpler than one under a slab or behind tile.

How many methods it takes

Pressure testing, acoustic listening, thermal imaging and tracer gas each add time.

Access

Finished walls and floors limit where the technician can listen and test.

Size of the system

A large house or a long service line takes longer to isolate.

Time of the call

Night, weekend and holiday calls carry extra dispatch cost.

Leak detection versus repair

Detection tells you where the leak is. Repair fixes it. Some companies bundle both, and some charge for detection and credit it toward the repair. Ask which applies. A plumber who finds the leak and then insists on a different company for the repair should be able to explain why.

Our leak detection service covers the full search, and a confirmed split line goes to burst pipe repair or water service line repair depending on where it sits. We also serve Rocky Hill and Glastonbury, and the emergency line is answered 24/7.

Does insurance help

Policies differ. Many cover sudden water damage but exclude gradual leaks, and some cover the cost of tearing out to reach a leak while excluding the pipe itself. Read your policy or ask your agent before work starts, and keep the leak report, since it documents the cause.

If the water bill jumps or the street is wet

MDC runs a Command Center for reporting a main break or a service leak at (860) 278-7850, press 1. MDC does not state on that page that the line is staffed around the clock, so call and confirm for your situation.

If you see water pooling in the street or in the curb area near the meter, call MDC first, because the leak may be on their side of the line. If the meter is spinning with every fixture off and the street is dry, the leak is likely on your side, and that is where leak detection starts.

Source type: MDC, report a main break or service leak page.

How to avoid paying twice

Before anyone starts, do the free checks yourself. Read the meter with everything off, check the toilets with dye and look under sinks and around the water heater. If the meter is still, the leak may be intermittent or inside a fixture, and a plumber can save time by knowing what you ruled out.

Keep a short log of what you saw. Note when the bill rose, when you heard water running and which rooms felt damp. A good technician uses that to choose where to listen first, which shortens the visit and keeps the cost down.

What you get at the end

  • The leak location, described or marked on the surface.
  • A note of which method found it.
  • A plain statement of what is leaking and why.
  • Repair options with a price for each, given before work begins.
  • A copy of the findings for your insurer if needed.

If a company will not tell you where the leak is until you sign a repair contract, ask why. Detection results belong to you, and a clear report lets you compare repair quotes from more than one company.

What to do while you wait for a leak visit

A leak you cannot stop

Limit the damage first, and then call.

  • Close the main valve, which MDC says is the valve nearest the meter.
  • Open the lowest tap to drain the line and relieve pressure.
  • Turn off power to any wet area before you touch anything electrical.
  • Move belongings out of the way of the water.
  • Take photos of the damage for your insurer before you clean up.

A note on warranty and insurance paperwork

If a leak caused damage, keep every document: the plumber's report, photos, receipts and the dates of each call. Insurers often ask when the problem began and what was done about it. A clear written report from the detection visit supports your claim and shows that you acted promptly once you knew.

If the leak is on a line you cannot see, ask the plumber to show you the tool readings or the thermal image. A good report lets you see what the plumber saw, and that builds trust before any repair is priced.

Ask your insurer before work starts whether they require their own adjuster to see the damage first. Some policies need that, and tearing out wet material before they see it can complicate the claim.

Code and permit note

Detection is diagnostic work with no permit. The repair after it follows the plumbing code, which in Connecticut is the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted by the 2022 State Building Code, and permit rules vary with the work.
