Before we arrive: your first moves in Newington

When water is moving where it should not, the minutes before the plumber arrives matter most. Work through these four steps while you are on the phone with us, and we will guide you through any that are unclear.

1

Shut off the main

Close the main water valve, usually in the basement near the meter, by turning it clockwise.

2

Kill power to wet areas

Switch off power to wet rooms only if you can reach the panel dry. Never stand in water to do it.

3

Clear the area

What makes Newington homes different

The Newington plan of conservation and development counts about 12,871 housing units in 2017 and says about 64 percent are single-family detached homes. It also shows the stock growing from 4,972 units in 1960 to 7,655 in 1970, so a large share of the town's homes are now more than half a century old.

That matters because the water heater, the supply lines and the drain lines in a house built in the 1960s are all at an age when problems cluster. We look at what each house has and tell you what is worth repairing now and what can wait.

The same plan lists about 8,383 acres of town land, with the Berlin Turnpike as a business corridor of roughly 155 acres of retail zoning. Shops and restaurants there make up a different kind of call, which is why we also take commercial plumbing work.

Water, sewer and the town

Newington is an MDC member town, so water and sewer mains are MDC's, and the lines from your house to them are yours. Town Hall is at 200 Garfield Street, with a general number of 860-665-8500. If an issue involves permits or the street, those are the first calls to make along with ours.

Piper Brook is mapped with a 100-year flood plain in the town's plan. If you live near a brook or a low corner, a working sump pump is worth checking before the next storm, and we can test it during a visit.
