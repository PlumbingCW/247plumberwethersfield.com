A planned installation is a chance to get the details right. Where the heater goes, how it vents, how it is supplied and how it drains all matter as much as the unit itself. We walk through those choices with you before ordering anything.

This page is for planned work. If your tank is leaking or you have no hot water now, see water heater replacement and call our 24/7 line.

What a good installation includes

Correct venting

A gas heater needs a flue that draws properly and combustion air that does not starve the burner.

Safe relief valve and drain

A temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe to a safe place is a safety device, not an option.

Expansion control

A closed system can need an expansion tank to absorb the pressure of heated water.

A pan and shutoffs

A drain pan where a leak could damage floors, and a shutoff valve at the heater, make the next problem smaller.

Where a new heater can go

Basements are the usual spot in Wethersfield, close to the main shutoff and the floor drain. Garages and utility closets work too, with the right clearances and venting. A heater in a finished room needs more thought about leaks and noise.

If you are moving the heater, the cost depends on how far the gas, water and vent lines must run. We price the move before we start and explain the choices that save money.

Gas, electric or heat pump

Fuel choice usually follows what is already in the house. A gas heater needs a gas line and a flue. An electric heater needs a dedicated circuit of the right size. A heat pump water heater uses electricity efficiently but needs space and a condensate drain. Our water heater repair page explains how each type fails, which is useful when you compare.

Code and permit note

Installing a water heater in a new spot means new piping, venting and sometimes new electrical or gas work. The 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), and a permit is more likely when the location or fuel changes. Call the Building Department at 860-721-2840 before work starts.
