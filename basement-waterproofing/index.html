Waterproofing is the work of keeping water out of a basement, or of moving it out quickly when it gets in. Which approach fits depends on where the water comes from, how often it comes and how the house was built.

If your basement is flooding right now, see sump pump repair and call our 24/7 line. This page is for planning a lasting fix.

Where basement water comes from

Groundwater through the floor

Rising groundwater pushes up through the slab and the joint where the floor meets the wall.

Wall seepage

Water moves through block, mortar joints or cracks after heavy rain.

Surface water

Poor grading, short downspouts and clogged gutters send water to the foundation.

Sewer or drain backup

Wastewater coming from a floor drain is a plumbing problem, not a waterproofing one.

Interior systems: how they work

An interior drainage system collects water at the base of the wall and carries it to a sump pit. A pump then sends it away from the house. The system does not stop water from reaching the wall, but it controls where it goes, and the basement stays dry.

This approach is often practical in a finished or heavily used basement, because it works from the inside and needs less excavation than exterior work. It is the plumber's side of waterproofing, and it relies on a working pump and a safe discharge.

The ground Wethersfield sits on

The town's 2013 plan notes that a flood in 1692 straightened the Connecticut River's course through the area and may have created Wethersfield Cove. The practical point is that this is river-edge ground, where groundwater can be high in wet seasons. A basement near the Cove or the river is more likely to see water rise in spring than one on higher ground.

MDC's Clean Water Project says that in heavy rain, sewer flow in Wethersfield climbs from about 1.7 million gallons a day to about 9.1 million, and that overflows can reach basements. That is a sewer issue, not a groundwater one, and it needs a different fix. We help you tell which you have.

What to do before calling

Take photos of the wet areas and note when the water appears, such as after heavy rain, after snowmelt or all the time. Check your gutters and downspouts, and make sure they carry water several feet away from the house. Those two checks answer a surprising share of the questions we ask on the phone.

A basement that gets wet only during storms often needs better drainage and a reliable pump. One that is damp all the time may have a different problem, such as a leaking pipe. Our leak detection service rules that out.

Code and permit note

Waterproofing touches drainage, the slab and the sump system, so parts of it fall under the plumbing code. The 2022 Connecticut State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), and permit rules vary with the work. Ask the Wethersfield Building Department before excavation starts.
