A drain that backs up again and again is more than an annoyance, so our line is answered 24/7. While you wait, stop using water in the affected fixtures and avoid chemical cleaners. Jetting is the right tool when the blockage keeps returning, and we help you decide on the phone whether it fits.

Hydro jetting sends water through a hose at high pressure to scour grease, scale, sludge and roots off the pipe wall. A snake pushes a hole through the clog. A jet cleans the pipe.

What jetting removes

Grease

Kitchen and restaurant lines coat with fat that a snake cannot scrape off the wall.

Scale and sludge

Mineral scale and soap residue narrow the pipe slowly until flow drops.

Roots

Fine roots are cut and flushed out. Heavy root masses may need a cutting head first.

Silt and debris

Fine sand and sediment settle in low spots and are flushed along the line.

How a jetting visit works

We access the line through a cleanout, a vent or a removed toilet, and feed a jetting hose with a specialized nozzle down the pipe. The nozzle pushes forward and sprays backward, so it cleans as it advances and pulls debris out toward the access point. We follow with a camera to confirm the wall is clean and to check for damage.

Jetting is not for every pipe. A pipe that is cracked, collapsed or badly corroded can be damaged by high pressure. The camera first tells us whether the pipe can take it, and we tell you plainly when it cannot.

Older sewer lines in Wethersfield

MDC says most of the 98 miles of sewer mains in its system are more than 50 years old. Homes of the same era can have older laterals, and older pipe can be fragile. We camera before we jet, so we do not push pressure into a pipe that cannot take it. When the camera shows damage, we recommend repair first.

Code and permit note

Jetting clears and scours a pipe, and it does not change the system, so no permit is usually in question. If the camera finds a defect, the repair follows the plumbing code, which in Connecticut is the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted by the 2022 State Building Code.
