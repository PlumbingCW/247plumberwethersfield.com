Tankless Flush With Vinegar: Wethersfield, CT Homeowner Guide

A vinegar flush removes scale from a tankless heater's heat exchanger. Here is the process and how often to do it.

Wethersfield guideLocal facts
Talk to a plumber
(860) 397-8103Call now

Written by the Wethersfield 24/7 Plumbing team. Published and updated November 14, 2026.

Scale builds up inside a tankless water heater and chokes the flow. A vinegar flush dissolves it, restores flow and helps the unit last. The job takes an hour or so and needs a small pump, two hoses and a few gallons of white vinegar.

Always follow your manufacturer's instructions first. They tell you the right solution, the flush time and whether your unit has the service valves that make a flush possible.

Signs your tankless heater needs a flush

Lower hot water flow

Scale narrows the passages and the unit cannot pass the same volume.

Error codes

Many units show an error when the heat exchanger is restricted.

Water that runs hot, then cold

The unit shuts down to protect itself when it overheats.

It has been a year or more

Many manufacturers call for an annual flush.

What you need

  • A small submersible pump, sometimes sold as a descaling kit.
  • Two hoses with fittings for the service valves.
  • A five gallon bucket.
  • About three gallons of undiluted white vinegar, or the descaling solution your manual names.
  • Gloves and eye protection.

How a flush goes

1

Power down

Turn off the electricity and the gas, and close the water valves above the unit.

2

Connect the hoses

Attach the hoses to the hot and cold service valves and run one to the pump and one to the bucket.

3

Circulate

Pour the vinegar in the bucket, open the valves and run the pump for about forty-five minutes to an hour.

4

Rinse

Drain the vinegar, flush the unit with clean water for a few minutes and clean the inlet filter.

5

Restart

Close the service valves, reopen the water and gas, restore power and check for leaks.

When a flush is not enough

If flow does not improve after a flush, or if the unit still shows errors, the cause may be a venting problem, a failed sensor or a damaged heat exchanger. A technician can test each. Our tankless page explains what we check, and our repair service covers the fixes.

If your unit has no service valves, a plumber can add them, which makes every future flush easier. We serve Newington and Glastonbury along with Wethersfield.

Wethersfield water and your tankless heater

MDC states in its FAQ that its water has a hardness of approximately 15 milligrams per liter, or 1 grain per gallon. That is on the soft end of the scale.

Soft water forms scale slowly, so a tankless heater here is less likely to clog quickly than one in a hard-water town. It still collects some, and the manufacturer's schedule exists for a reason. Check the inlet filter and flush on the interval your manual gives.

Source type: MDC water hardness FAQ.

Check the inlet filter and the vent

Many tankless heaters have a small mesh screen where the cold water enters. It catches grit, and when it clogs it cuts the flow to the unit and triggers errors that look like scale. Pull the filter during each flush, rinse it and put it back. It takes two minutes and often fixes a flow complaint by itself.

The vent and the air intake matter just as much on gas units. A blocked or damaged vent can shut the heater down, and a flue problem is a safety issue, not a nuisance. If your unit shows a vent or ignition code, do not keep resetting it. Call a plumber or the manufacturer's service line.

Safety notes for a flush

Turn off the gas and the power

Do this before touching any valve or hose.

Wear gloves and eye protection

Vinegar is mild, but splashes still sting.

Do not mix vinegar with other cleaners

Mixing products can produce harmful fumes.

Rinse thoroughly

Run clean water through the unit until the vinegar smell is gone, so it does not reach a kettle or a glass.

Use the right solution

Some manufacturers name a specific descaler. Use what the manual says.

If any step feels uncertain, stop and call. A flush is a simple job, but a mistake with a gas or electric appliance is not worth saving an hour.

How the flush fits into a yearly routine

Pick a date you will remember, such as the start of fall, and do the flush then. Pair it with the inlet filter check and a glance at the vent and the gas connection. Write the date on a label near the unit, so the next person who looks, you or a plumber, knows when it was last done.

If you cannot recall the last flush, do not wait for symptoms. Scale builds out of sight, and by the time flow drops or an error shows, the exchanger may already be restricted. A flush done a little early costs an hour and a few gallons of vinegar. A flush done late can mean a service call, or a heat exchanger that no vinegar will clear.

Keep the manual handy and note the model and serial number. When you call a plumber, those two items let them look up the right parts and the right flush procedure before they arrive.

Code and permit note

A flush is maintenance, and the manufacturer's manual governs it. Replacing or relocating a tankless unit brings in the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), as adopted by Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code, along with fuel and venting rules.
