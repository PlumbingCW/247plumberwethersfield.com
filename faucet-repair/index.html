A valve that will not close: what to do now

Steps for a leaking or stuck valve

Shutoff valves that have sat for years can fail when you turn them.

  • Turn the valve gently clockwise. Forcing it can break the stem and release a flood.
  • If it will not close, shut the main water valve and call us.
  • Place a bucket under the leak and a towel around the base to protect the cabinet.
  • Open a lower faucet to drain the line once the water is off.

Faucet and sink trouble, sorted

Drips from the spout

A worn cartridge, washer or seat lets water slip by. The fix is a small part and a short visit.

Leaks at the handle or base

A worn O-ring or packing lets water escape around the stem or under the faucet.

Low flow or spray

A clogged aerator or a failing cartridge reduces flow. Cleaning or replacing the part restores it.

Leaks under the sink

Supply lines, valves and drain connections are the likely sources, and each is a different repair.

Our approach to fixtures

We find the source first. A drip from the spout is a faucet issue. Water on the cabinet floor is usually a supply line, a valve or a drain connection. We check each, repair what is failing and test with the water on. If the faucet is old and the parts are no longer made, we explain your replacement options.

We also replace angle stops and supply lines, the small parts that cause big floods. Replacing them while we are there is cheap insurance against a call at midnight.

Outdoor faucets and winter

Outdoor faucets are a frequent winter casualty. A hose left connected holds water against the faucet and can freeze inside the wall. MDC notes that the shutoff for an outdoor faucet is usually within about 3 feet of the interior basement wall. Close it in the fall, open the outdoor spigot to drain, and a lot of winter trouble goes away.

A frost-proof faucet needs a slight slope to drain, so a worn or poorly installed one can still freeze. If your outdoor faucet drips inside the house after a freeze, close its shutoff and call us.

Faucet parts in Wethersfield homes

MDC states that its water is soft, at about 1 grain per gallon. That is easier on faucet cartridges and aerators than hard water, which clogs them with scale. Still, older fixtures in a house from before 1950 may use parts that are hard to find, and we tell you when replacement is the practical choice.

Code and permit note

A faucet repair is routine and usually needs no permit. Moving or adding a faucet is a different matter, since the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), as adopted by Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code, sets rules for supply lines, shutoffs and backflow protection on outdoor spigots.
