A drain that is backing up can flood a floor in minutes, so we answer the line 24/7. While you wait, stop running water in the house, do not flush, and do not pour chemical cleaners into a drain that is already full. They can splash back and they make the plumber's work harder.

Most clogs are in one fixture and clear quickly. A clog that affects several fixtures at once is a different problem, usually in the main drain, and that is where the cost and the urgency change.

Act first

Backed-up drain: what to do first

First steps for a backed-up drain

The key is to find out how big the problem is.

  • Check other fixtures. If only one is slow, the clog is probably in that branch.
  • If a tub, shower or floor drain fills when you flush a toilet or run a washer, the main drain is likely blocked.
  • Stop running water everywhere. Every gallon that goes down has nowhere to go.
  • Keep children and pets out of any standing water. If it looks or smells like sewage, treat it as contaminated.

Where clogs form

Kitchen sinks

Grease cools and hardens in the pipe, and food scraps catch on the film. Slow draining that returns a week after a clearing points to a grease buildup.

Bathroom sinks and tubs

Hair and soap form a mat just below the stopper or in the trap. These are usually the quickest clogs to clear.

Toilets

Too much paper or a foreign object is the usual cause. A toilet that keeps clogging may have a deeper issue in the line.

Main drain

Roots, a belly in the pipe, or years of buildup block the line to the street. Several fixtures drain slowly or gurgle at once.

How we clear a drain

We start with the least invasive tool that will do the job. A hand auger or cable machine clears most sink and tub clogs. A larger cable with a cutting head handles roots and hard buildup in main lines. When grease or scale coats the pipe wall, a snake only punches a hole, and hydro jetting cleans the pipe itself.

After a main drain clearing, we recommend a camera look when the problem is a repeat. A sewer camera inspection shows whether the clog was a one-off or a symptom of a broken or sagging pipe.

Kitchen sink clogs and what to stop pouring

Kitchen drains clog from fat, coffee grounds and starchy food that swells in the pipe. Hot water helps for a moment and then the grease cools farther down the line. If your kitchen sink slows every few weeks, the pipe is probably lined with buildup, and a one-time jetting often ends the cycle.

Baking soda and vinegar will freshen a drain but will not clear a real clog. Use them for odor, not for a real blockage.

Wethersfield drains and the town sewer

MDC provides sewer service in Wethersfield, and its Clean Water Project says most of the 98 miles of sewer mains in the system are more than 50 years old, with some dating to 1914. Those mains are MDC's, while the line from your house to the street is often the owner's to maintain, so confirm the boundary with MDC. A backup that affects the whole house is worth checking against both.

If a camera shows the blockage is in the street main, we will tell you and point you to MDC. If it is on your side, we clear it and explain the options. The distinction saves you from paying to fix someone else's pipe.

Code and permit note

Clearing a drain is maintenance, but any fix that changes the pipe falls under the plumbing code. Connecticut's current State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which governs traps, vents and drain slopes. If a clog traces to a bad vent or a flat run, the repair is a code matter.
