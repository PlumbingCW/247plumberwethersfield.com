Leaking or jammed: what to do now

Safe steps for a bad disposal

A disposal combines water, electricity and sharp blades.

  • Turn off the switch and unplug the unit, or switch off its breaker, before touching anything.
  • Use a hex key in the socket on the underside to turn the blades back and forth to free a jam.
  • Press the red reset button on the underside after it cools.
  • If water is leaking, put a bucket under the unit and call us. Do not run the dishwasher.

Why a disposal fails

It hums but does not spin

Something is jamming the blades, often a bone, a fruit pit or a utensil.

It does nothing

A tripped reset or a failed motor. A reset button can restore it, and a dead motor means replacement.

It leaks

A leak at the top seal, the dishwasher hose or the drain connection is common. A leak from the housing means the unit has failed.

It smells or drains slowly

Buildup in the grinding chamber or a clog in the drain below.

What we do

We check the power, the jam and the leak path. A jam is cleared, a loose connection is tightened and a worn seal is replaced. If the motor is dead or the housing has cracked, we recommend a new disposal, and we install it with fresh connections and test it.

A disposal that backs up the sink may have a clog beyond it. We clear the line and check the dishwasher drain. If grease is the cause, our drain cleaning service can clean the line.

Habits that save a disposal

Run cold water before, during and after use so fats stay solid and move through. Skip grease, coffee grounds, egg shells, celery strings and fibrous peels in quantity. A disposal is not a trash can, and the drain below it is the weak link.

A disposal that is used every day in a busy kitchen wears out in time. When yours does, we replace it with a unit sized to your household. See our plumbing installation page for what a clean installation involves.

Code and permit note

A disposal swap is simple, but the drain connection and the electrical supply are both regulated. Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which covers the trap and drain. Wiring work has its own rules, and a licensed trade handles it.
