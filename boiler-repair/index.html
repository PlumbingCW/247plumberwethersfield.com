No heat: what to check first

Safe checks before you call

Some boiler faults are simple. Others should be left to a professional.

  • Raise the thermostat and confirm it clicks on. Check the batteries if it is digital.
  • Look at the boiler's pressure gauge. Normal is usually in the middle of the range. Do not add water unless you know how.
  • Check the breaker and the switch near the boiler.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first.

Why boilers stop or misbehave

No heat or no hot water

A failed circulator pump, a tripped limit or a faulty thermostat can stop heat from reaching the house.

Banging, knocking or kettling

Sediment, trapped air or a failing pump can cause noise as water moves or boils.

Pressure drops

A leak in the system or a failed expansion tank lets pressure fall, and the boiler will shut off.

Short cycling

A boiler that turns on and off rapidly may be oversized, have a faulty control or be starved of fuel or air.

What we do and where we stop

We diagnose the fault by testing the controls, the circulator, the pressure, the expansion tank and the system's air and water. Many faults are small, such as a bad zone valve or a thermostat. If the problem is a burner or a combustion issue beyond our scope, we tell you and help you find the right specialist.

We also help with the plumbing side of heating, such as leaks in the loop, bleeding air from radiators and replacing valves. If a leak has been feeding the system with fresh water, it shortens the life of the boiler, and finding it matters.

A yearly boiler service

A yearly service catches small problems before the first cold night. It covers a check of the controls, the circulator, the expansion tank and pressure, a look for leaks, and a check of the combustion side where it is part of the visit. Book it in early fall and the heating season starts with fewer surprises.

Boilers in older Wethersfield houses

About 27 percent of Wethersfield's housing was built before 1950, according to the 2019 town profile, and the town's stock leans toward single-family detached homes. Many older New England houses are heated by hydronic systems with cast iron radiators or baseboards, and some boilers are decades old. An old boiler that still runs is not a problem. An old boiler that is leaking or short cycling deserves a close look.

If your system is losing pressure, a hidden leak in the loop can be the cause, and our leak detection work covers heating loops as well as domestic water. A leaking boiler is also a reason to look at your water heater, since the two often sit side by side in the basement.

Code and permit note

Boiler work involves fuel, venting and pressure vessels, which carry their own rules beyond the plumbing code. Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) for the piping, and permit rules vary with the work. Call the Building Department at 860-721-2840 if you plan a replacement.
