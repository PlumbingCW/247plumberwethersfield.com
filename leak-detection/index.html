A leak you cannot see is urgent because it keeps working while you wait. Our line is answered 24/7, so call as soon as you suspect one. While you wait for a plumber, shut off the main water valve, stop using the fixtures you can, and keep people and electronics away from any wet floor or ceiling.

Leak detection is the work of finding the exact spot before anything is opened. A good trace turns a vague worry, a high bill or a stain on a ceiling, into a small, targeted repair. A bad guess can mean cutting three walls to find one fitting.

Act first

When a hidden leak is an emergency

Do these four things while you wait

Water and electricity do not mix, and hidden water often sits near both.

  • Close the main valve. In most houses it is where the pipe enters the basement, and the MDC meter sits nearby.
  • Stay out of any room with a sagging ceiling. Wet drywall can drop without warning.
  • Do not touch light switches or outlets in a wet area. Turn off that circuit at the panel only if the panel is dry and easy to reach.
  • Take a photo of the damage and the meter reading. Both help later if you make an insurance claim.

Signs that water is escaping where you cannot see it

A bill that climbed with no new habit

A steady rise month over month, with the same people and the same laundry, usually means water is leaving the system.

The sound of running water in a quiet house

A faint hiss or trickle when every fixture is off often points to a pressurized line leaking inside a wall or under a slab.

Warm spots on the floor

A patch of floor that feels warm, or a hot water line that heats slowly, can mean a hot pipe is leaking below.

Musty smells, stains or bubbling paint

Moisture behind a wall or above a ceiling shows up as discoloration and a smell long before it drips.

How we find a leak without tearing the house apart

We begin with the meter. With every fixture closed, a moving dial or flickering leak indicator tells us water is leaving the house, and it also tells us how fast. Then we split the system into sections by closing valves, so we can see which part of the house or yard the water is leaving from.

Once the section is known, we narrow it with listening equipment, pressure testing and, where it helps, thermal imaging for hot lines. The goal is a mark on the floor or wall that says the leak is here, within a small area. Only then do we talk about opening anything, and you see the price before we do.

Where leaks hide in Wethersfield homes

About 27 percent of the town's housing was built before 1950, according to the 2019 town profile. Pipes that old can corrode from the inside and pinhole, and a pinhole spray inside a wall can run for weeks. Newer homes leak differently, usually at fittings, appliance connections or a line damaged during a renovation. Our guide to plumbing in Old Wethersfield covers what to ask when the house is much older.

Basements hide most of the supply and drain lines in this part of Connecticut, so many leaks are easy to reach once they are located. Leaks under a concrete slab, in an addition or beneath a garage floor are harder, and they are where listening equipment earns its cost.

Call now

High water bill or meter still running

A high bill is one of the most common reasons people call us about leaks. Before you phone, try a simple test. Turn off every tap, appliance and ice maker, then look at the meter. If the leak indicator turns or the dial moves, water is leaving the house, and the next question is where.

The usual suspects are a toilet that leaks silently into the bowl, a water heater relief valve that drips, an irrigation line or a buried service line. Our toilet repair and water service line repair pages cover the two most frequent culprits.

MDC states that if you find a main break or service leak, you should shut the valve nearest the meter and call a plumber. It also lists a Command Center line at (860) 278-7850, press 1, for reporting a main break or service leak. If the leak is on MDC's side of the meter, they are the ones who repair it, and a correct diagnosis saves a wasted visit.

Slab, under-house and underground leaks

Leaks beneath concrete or in the ground do not announce themselves. A slab leak can show as a warm floor or a bill that climbs for no reason. A buried line leak can show as a soggy patch in the lawn that stays wet in dry weather. We use the same meter and section process, then listen along the suspected path to place the leak.

When the leak is in a line that is old, corroded or repeatedly failing, spot repair may not be the best use of your money. We will tell you when a short section can be fixed and when repiping makes more sense, and you decide with the numbers in front of you.

Leaks at businesses

Restaurants, offices and shops along Silas Deane Highway and the Berlin Turnpike lose money when water is lost and when a leak closes the doors. Commercial leak calls follow the same process, with extra attention to shutoffs, tenant space and after-hours access. Our commercial plumbing page explains how we handle those calls.

Code and permit note

Finding a leak is a diagnostic job, but the repair that follows is regulated. Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), so a replaced section of supply pipe is expected to meet that code's materials rules. Ask the Wethersfield Building Department whether your repair needs a permit.
