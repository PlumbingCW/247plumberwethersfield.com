Boiler Repair and Servicing for Wethersfield, CT Homes

A boiler that is serviced yearly lasts longer and fails less often. Here is what service includes and which faults mean a call.

Wethersfield guideLocal facts

Written by the Wethersfield 24/7 Plumbing team. Published and updated November 14, 2026.

A boiler heats water or makes steam and sends it through radiators, baseboard or radiant floors. It is a closed system with a burner, a heat exchanger, a pump or a steam supply and a set of safety controls. Annual service keeps it efficient, and a quick look at the common faults saves a lot of cold mornings.

This guide covers what service involves, the faults that homeowners notice and the ones that need a plumber or a heating technician. Gas smell and carbon monoxide alarms are always an emergency, so we start there.

Safety comes first

Gas smell or carbon monoxide alarm

These are emergencies. Do not try to repair anything.

  • Leave the house with everyone and pets.
  • Do not use switches, phones or flames inside.
  • Call 911 and the gas utility from outside.
  • Do not go back in until the fire department says so.

What a boiler service includes

1

Visual check

Look for leaks, rust, scorching and corrosion on the boiler and piping.

2

Burner and combustion

Inspect the burner, clean as needed and check combustion and the flue.

3

Controls and safeties

Test the thermostat, limit controls, pressure relief valve and low water cutoff.

4

Pressure and water

Check system pressure, the expansion tank and the water level.

5

Bleed and balance

Bleed air from radiators and check that each zone heats evenly.

Common boiler faults

No heat

A tripped safety, a failed circulator or a dead thermostat is common.

Banging or kettling

Scale or low water flow can make the heat exchanger noisy.

Pressure that drops

A slow leak or a failed expansion tank is the usual cause.

Radiators cold at the top

Air is trapped and needs to be bled.

Pilot or ignition that fails

A dirty sensor or a gas supply issue can stop ignition.

When to repair and when to replace

A boiler that needs frequent repairs, leaks from the heat exchanger or fails a combustion test may be near the end of its life. Replacement adds cost but it also buys efficiency and safety. Ask for a plain comparison of repair against replacement, and ask what is likely to fail next.

Older housing means older heat

The town's 2019 profile reports that 26.8 percent of Wethersfield's housing was built before 1950 and that 79 percent of homes are owner occupied. The 2013 plan adds that the 1950s brought about one new house per day.

The tie back for you is practical. Houses of that age often have boilers with long histories, radiators or baseboard that have been added to over time and piping that has been patched. A service visit finds the weak points before the first cold week, and an owner who stays in the home has the most to gain from keeping it running.

Steam, hot water and radiant systems

Boilers fall into a few families. Steam systems boil water and send steam up to radiators, which then condense and run back down. Hot water systems send heated water through baseboard or radiators and return it to the boiler. Radiant systems run warm water through tubing in the floor. Each has its own faults and its own service needs, so a technician has to know which one you have.

Steam systems depend on the right water level and on air vents that let air out of the radiators. A stuck vent or a low water cutoff that needs flushing can stop the heat. Hot water systems depend on the circulator, the expansion tank and the air removal. A system that makes gurgling noises usually needs air bled out.

Cold weather habits that help

  • Keep the thermostat steady instead of swinging the setting up and down.
  • Keep radiators and baseboard clear of furniture and drapes.
  • Note the boiler pressure gauge reading when the system is working normally.
  • Know where the shutoff for the boiler and the gas are.
  • Keep the area around the boiler clear of storage.

A boiler that fails on the coldest night is a stressful call. Annual service, and a short list of the habits above, cut the odds of that call considerably.

A simple pre-winter routine

Set aside an afternoon in early fall. Turn the heat on before you need it, and let each zone run for a while. Walk the house and feel every radiator or baseboard run, top to bottom. A cold spot tells you where air is trapped or flow is blocked. Check the pressure gauge against the number you noted last year, and look at the floor under the boiler for drips or rust marks.

Then listen. A healthy boiler hums and clicks as it starts. A new rattle, a bang when the burner lights or a gurgle in the pipes is worth a call before the first cold snap, because parts are easier to get and appointments are easier to book in October than in January.

Replace the batteries in your carbon monoxide alarms at the same time, and make sure there is one on every level near the sleeping areas. It is the cheapest safety step in the house.

Code and permit note

Service is maintenance, and a replacement is a permitted project in most towns. Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) for the piping, and fuel and venting have their own rules. Ask the Building Department what applies.
